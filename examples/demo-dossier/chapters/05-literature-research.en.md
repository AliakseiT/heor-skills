# 05. Literature Research

## Search strategy

A systematic literature search was conducted on 15 January 2026 across two
databases: PubMed (via NCBI E-utilities) and ClinicalTrials.gov (v2 API).
The search used a narrow-tier strategy with precision-first design: MeSH
terms and title/abstract concept blocks combined with an RCT hedge for
PubMed, and structured AREA[] field queries for ClinicalTrials.gov.

The PICO framework guided the search:
- **Population:** Adults 18-65 with mild to moderate depression (PHQ-9 5-14)
- **Intervention:** Guided digital CBT (SereniCBT, 12-week programme)
- **Comparison:** Standard outpatient care without a digital intervention
- **Outcomes:** Depression severity (PHQ-9), response rate, remission, quality of life (EQ-5D-5L), healthcare utilisation

Two mandatory references were provided by the applicant (Mueller et al. 2024
and Huber et al. 2024 cost-effectiveness study) and resolved by DOI.

## Included studies

Six studies were included in the synthesis, ranked by relevance and
seminal importance:

1. **Mueller et al. 2024** [1] — Multicentre RCT (n=312) of SereniCBT in 8
   Swiss primary care sites. The pivotal trial for this dossier, reporting a
   PHQ-9 reduction of -3.2 (95% CI -4.1 to -2.3) vs standard care, response
   rate 61% vs 38%, and remission rate 44% vs 23%.

2. **Andersson et al. 2023** [2] — Systematic review and meta-analysis of 42
   RCTs (n=6,891) comparing guided vs unguided internet-based CBT. Guided
   iCBT showed a large effect (d=0.78, 95% CI 0.65-0.91) with lower dropout
   (18% vs 47%). Cost per QALY ranged from EUR 2,400 to EUR 8,900.

3. **Huber et al. 2024** [3] — Swiss payer-perspective cost-utility study
   using a Markov model. Digital CBT had an ICER of CHF 8,200 per QALY
   (95% CI 3,100-15,400) with a 92% probability of cost-effectiveness at
   a CHF 50,000 WTP threshold.

4. **Richards et al. 2023** [4] — Pragmatic RCT (n=634) of a mobile CBT app
   in UK primary care. PHQ-9 difference -1.2 (95% CI -1.9 to -0.5), EQ-5D-5L
   improvement 0.045, and a reduction in GP visits of 1.4 per 6 months.

5. **Kolovos et al. 2023** [5] — 12-month follow-up of a digital CBT trial
   (n=278). Effect size remained significant at 12 months (d=0.52), with a
   relapse rate of 19% vs 34% (HR 0.51).

6. **Fischer et al. 2023** [6] — Pooled adherence analysis of 4 RCTs
   (n=1,205). Module completion averaged 64%, with adherence-outcome
   correlation r=0.31 (p<0.001).

## Evidence summary

The evidence base supports the effectiveness of guided digital CBT for
mild-to-moderate depression in primary care. The pivotal trial [1] directly
evaluates SereniCBT in the Swiss setting, demonstrating clinically
significant improvements in depression severity, response, and remission
rates. The meta-analysis [2] confirms that guided interventions outperform
unguided ones, with lower dropout and larger effect sizes. The long-term
follow-up [5] provides durability data showing sustained effects at 12
months with reduced relapse rates, which informs the Markov model's
transition probabilities.

The cost-effectiveness evidence [3] directly addresses the Swiss OKP
perspective, reporting an ICER well below conventional willingness-to-pay
thresholds. The pragmatic trial [4] corroborates findings in a different
healthcare system, and the adherence analysis [6] provides parameter
estimates for the economic model.
